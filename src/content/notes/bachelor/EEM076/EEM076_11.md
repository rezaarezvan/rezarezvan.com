### Equivalent Impedance
Given the circuit and that $\omega = 5 \ \mathrm{rad/s}$ - find the equivalent output impedance:

![Circuit for the equivalent impedance example.](./imgs/eq_impedance.svg "fig:eq-impedance")

As we discussed in the last part, our resistor rules for series and parallel apply for impedance as well.

Let's first quickly convert the capacitor and inductor to Ohm.

For our capacitor:
$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{C} & = \dfrac{1}{j\omega C} \newline
& = -j \cdot \dfrac{1}{\omega C} \newline
& = -j \cdot \dfrac{1}{5 \cdot 500 \cdot 10^{-3}} \ [\Omega] \newline
& = -j0.4 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

Same procedure for our inductor:

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{L} & = j\omega L \newline
& = j(5 \cdot 2) \ [\Omega] \newline
& = j10 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means we have the following circuit now:

![The same circuit with the impedances.](./imgs/eq_impedance_phasor.svg "fig:eq-impedance-phasor")

Now, let's simplify our impedance!

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{6 \parallel -j0.4} & = \dfrac{(6)(-j0.4)}{6 - j0.4} \newline
& = \dfrac{-j2.4}{6 - j0.4} \newline
& = \dfrac{(-j2.4)(6 + j0.4)}{(6 - j0.4)(6 + j0.4)} \newline
& = \dfrac{(-j2.4)(6 + j0.4)}{36.16} \newline
& = \dfrac{0.96 - j14.4}{36.16} \newline
& = \dfrac{0.96 - j14.4}{36.16 \angle 0^{\circ}} \newline
& = \dfrac{14.43 \angle -86.18^{\circ}}{36.16 \angle 0^{\circ}} \newline
& = \dfrac{14.43}{36.16} \angle -86.18^{\circ} \newline
& = 0.399 \angle -86.18^{\circ} \newline
& = 0.026 - j0.39 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{\text{series}} & = -j + j10 + 0.026 - j0.39 \newline
& = 0.026 + j8.61 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{10 \parallel 0.026 + j8.61} & = \dfrac{(10)(0.026 + j8.61)}{10 + 0.026 + j8.61} \newline
& = \dfrac{0.26 + j86.1}{10.026 + j8.61} \newline
& = \dfrac{86.1 \angle 89.82^{\circ}}{13.21 \angle 40.65^{\circ}} \newline
& = \dfrac{86.1}{13.21} \angle (89.82^{\circ} - 40.65^{\circ}) \newline
& = \boxed{6.51 \angle 49.17^{\circ} \ \Omega}.
\end{align*}
$$

### Multiple sources
Given this circuit, and that $\omega = 2 \ \mathrm{rad/s}$, $I_{c} = 2 \angle 28^{\circ}$ - find $\mathbf{I}_S$ and $I(t)$.

![Circuit with multiple sources.](./imgs/multiple_sources.svg "fig:multiple-sources")

Here we can utilize Ohm's law,

$$
\begin{align*}
V_{c} & = I_{c} Z_{c} \newline
& = \dfrac{1}{j\omega C} I_{c} \newline
& = -j \dfrac{1}{\omega C} I_{c} \newline
& = -j \dfrac{1}{2} (2 \angle 28^{\circ}) \newline
& = (-j0.5)(2 \angle 28^{\circ}) \newline
& = \left(\dfrac{1}{2} \angle -90^{\circ}\right)(2 \angle 28^{\circ}) \newline
& = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \angle (-90^{\circ} + 28^{\circ}) \newline
& = 1 \angle -62^{\circ}.
\end{align*}
$$

We know that $V_{R_2} = V_{C}$ since they are in parallel!

$$
\begin{align*}
I_{R_2} & = \dfrac{V_{R_2}}{Z_{R_{2}}} \newline
& = \dfrac{V_{C}}{Z_{R_{2}}} \newline
& = \dfrac{V_{C}}{2} \newline
& = \dfrac{1}{2} \angle -62^{\circ}.
\end{align*}
$$

Using Kirchhoff's current law we know that,

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{I}_S & = I_{R_2} + I_{C} \newline
& = \left(\dfrac{1}{2} \angle -62^{\circ}\right) + (2 \angle 28^{\circ}) \newline
& = (0.23 - j0.44) + (1.76 + j0.93) \newline
& = 1.99 + j0.49 \newline
& = \boxed{2.04 \angle 13.83^{\circ}}.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means that $I(t)$,

$$
\boxed{I(t) = 2.04 \cos(2t + 13.83^{\circ}) \ \mathrm{A}}.
$$

### Node Analysis

Given this circuit, find $V_1$ and $V_2$:

![Circuit for node analysis.](./imgs/node_analysis.svg "fig:node-analysis")

Let's perform our node analysis algorithm here:

For $V_1$,

$$
\begin{align*}
& -1 \angle 0^{\circ} + \dfrac{V_1}{5} + \dfrac{V_1}{-j10} + \dfrac{V_1 - V_2}{-j5} + \dfrac{V_1 - V_2}{j10} = 0 \newline
& \dfrac{V_1}{5} + j\dfrac{V_1}{10} + j\dfrac{V_1 - V_2}{5} - j\dfrac{V_1 - V_2}{10} = 1 \newline
& 0.2V_1 + j0.1V_1 + j0.2(V_1 - V_2) - j0.1(V_1 - V_2) = 1 \newline
& 0.2V_1 + j0.1V_1 + j0.2V_1 - j0.2V_2 -j0.1V_1 + j0.1V_2 = 1 \newline
& 0.2V_1 + j0.2V_1 - j0.1V_2 = 1 \newline
& (0.2 + j0.2)V_1 - j0.1V_2 = 1 \newline
& (0.2 + j0.2)V_1 = 1 + j0.1V_2 \newline
& V_1 = \dfrac{1 + j0.1V_2}{0.2 + j0.2}.
\end{align*}
$$

For $V_2$,

$$
\begin{align*}
& \dfrac{V_2 - V_1}{-j5} + \dfrac{V_2 - V_1}{j10} + \dfrac{V_2}{j5} + \dfrac{V_2}{10} + 0.5 \angle 90^{\circ} = 0 \newline
& j\dfrac{V_2 - V_1}{5} - j\dfrac{V_2 - V_1}{10} - \dfrac{V_2}{5} + \dfrac{V_2}{10} - j0.5 = 0 \newline
& j0.2(V_2 - V_1) - j0.1(V_2 - V_1) - 0.2V_2 + 0.1V_2 - j0.5 = 0 \newline
& j0.2V_2 - j0.2V_1 - j0.1V_2 + j0.1V_1 - 0.2V_2 + 0.1V_2 - j0.5 = 0 \newline
& -0.1V_2 + j0.1V_2 - j0.1V_1 = j0.5 \newline
& (0.1 - j0.1)V_2 - j0.1V_1 = j0.5 \newline
& V_2 = \dfrac{j0.5 + j0.1V_1}{0.1 - j0.1}.
\end{align*}
$$

Now finding $V_1$ and $V_2$, by substituting $V_1$ in this,

$$
\begin{align*}
V_2 & = \ldots = -2 + j4 \ \mathrm{V} \newline
& = \boxed{4.47 \angle 116.6^{\circ} \ \mathrm{V}}.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means $V_2(t)$,

$$
V_2(t) = \boxed{4.47 \cos(\omega t + 116.6^{\circ})}.
$$

For $V_1$,

$$
\begin{align*}
V_1 & = \ldots = 1 - j2 \ \mathrm{V} \newline
& = \boxed{2.24 \angle -63.4^{\circ} \ \mathrm{V}}.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means $V_1(t)$,

$$
V_1(t) = \boxed{2.24 \cos(\omega t - 63.4^{\circ})}.
$$

### Mesh analysis

Given this circuit, find $I_1(t)$ and $I_2(t)$:

![Circuit for mesh analysis.](./imgs/mesh_analysis.svg "fig:mesh-analysis")

Let's first quickly convert our capacitor and inductor:

Our inductor:

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{L} & = j\omega L \newline
& = j(10^3 \cdot 4 \cdot 10^{-3}) \ [\Omega] \newline
& = j4 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

Our capacitor:

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{C} & = -j \dfrac{1}{\omega C} \newline
& = -j \dfrac{1}{10^3 \cdot 500 \cdot 10^{-6}} \newline
& = -j2 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

Which gives us:

![The same circuit with the impedances and mesh currents.](./imgs/mesh_analysis_phasor.svg "fig:mesh-analysis-phasor")

For Mesh 1,

$$
\begin{align*}
-10 \angle 0^{\circ} + 3I_1 + j4(I_1 - I_2) & = 0 \newline
(3 + j4)I_1 - j4I_2 & = 10.
\end{align*}
$$

For Mesh 2,

$$
\begin{align*}
j4(I_2 - I_1) - j2I_2 + 2I_1 & = 0 \newline
(2 - j4)I_1 + j2I_2 & = 0.
\end{align*}
$$

Finding $I_1$ and $I_2$,

$$
\begin{align*}
I_1 & = \dfrac{14 + j8}{13} \newline
& = \boxed{1.24 \angle 29.7^{\circ} \ \mathrm{A}},
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
I_2 & = \dfrac{20 + j30}{13} \newline
& = \boxed{2.77 \angle 56.3^{\circ} \ \mathrm{A}}.
\end{align*}
$$

Meaning that,

$$
\begin{align*}
I_1(t) & = 1.24 \cos(10^3 t + 29.7^{\circ}), \newline
I_2(t) & = 2.77 \cos(10^3 t + 56.3^{\circ}).
\end{align*}
$$

### Superposition
Looking at this circuit again:

![Circuit for node analysis.](./imgs/node_analysis.svg)

One can see that we can solve this via the principle of superposition.

We first need to simplify our circuit impedance,

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{-j5 \parallel j10} & = \dfrac{(-j5)(j10)}{-j5 + j10} \newline
& = \dfrac{50}{j5} \newline
& = \dfrac{50 \angle 0^{\circ}}{j5} \newline
& = \dfrac{50 \angle 0^{\circ}}{5 \angle 90^{\circ}} \newline
& = \dfrac{50}{5} \angle -90^{\circ} \newline
& = 10 \angle -90^{\circ} \newline
& = -j10 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{5 \parallel -j10} & = \dfrac{(5)(-j10)}{5 - j10} \newline
& = \dfrac{(5)(-j10)(5 + j10)}{(5 - j10)(5 + j10)} \newline
& = \dfrac{(-j50)(5 + j10)}{5^2 + 10^2} \newline
& = \dfrac{500 - j250}{125} \newline
& = 4 - j2 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

$$
\begin{align*}
Z_{j5 \parallel 10} & = \ldots = 2 + j4 \ \Omega.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means:

![The simplified circuit.](./imgs/superposition.svg "fig:superposition")

When right source is off,

$$
\begin{align*}
& (4 - j2) \parallel (-j10 + 2 + j4) \newline
V_{1L} & = 1 \angle 0^{\circ} \dfrac{(4 - j2)(-j10 + 2 + j4)}{(4 - j2) + (-j10 + 2 + j4)} \newline
& = \ldots \newline
& = 2 - j2 \ \mathrm{V}.
\end{align*}
$$

Left source turned off,

$$
\begin{align*}
& (4 - j2 - j10) \parallel (2 + j4) \newline
V_{1R} & = (-0.5 \angle -90^{\circ}) \left(\dfrac{2 + j4}{4 - j2 - j10 + 2 + j4}\right) (4 - j2) \newline
& = \ldots \newline
& = -1 \ \mathrm{V}.
\end{align*}
$$

Which means,

$$
\begin{align*}
V_1 & = V_{1L} + V_{1R} \newline
& = 2 - j2 - 1 \newline
& = 1 - j2 \ \mathrm{V}.
\end{align*}
$$

Same procedure for $V_2$.

### Resonance
:::definition[Resonance]
A network is in resonance (or resonant) when the
voltage and current at the network input
terminals are in phase.
:::

A maximum-amplitude response is produced in
the resonant condition.

![Series resonant circuit.](./imgs/resonance.svg "fig:resonance")

From this we can see that,

$$
Z_{s} = j2\pi f L + R - j \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}.
$$

At the **resonant** frequency,

$$
\begin{align*}
j2\pi f_0 L & = j \dfrac{1}{2\pi f_0 C}, \newline
f_0 & = \dfrac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}.
\end{align*}
$$

We call the ratio of the reactance of the impedance to the resistance at the resonant frequency the **quality factor**,

$$
Q_{s} = \dfrac{2\pi f_0 L}{R} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f_0 CR}.
$$

Which means,

$$
Z_{s} = R\left[1 + jQ_s \left(\dfrac{f}{f_0} - \dfrac{f_0}{f}\right)\right].
$$

$$
I = \dfrac{V_s}{Z_s} = \dfrac{V_s}{R\left[1 + jQ_s \left(\dfrac{f}{f_0} - \dfrac{f_0}{f}\right)\right]}
$$

$$
RI = \dfrac{V_s}{1 + jQ_s \left(\dfrac{f}{f_0} - \dfrac{f_0}{f}\right)}
$$

$$
\dfrac{V_R}{V_s} = \dfrac{1}{1 + jQ_s \left(\dfrac{f}{f_0} - \dfrac{f_0}{f}\right)}
$$

$$
B = f_H - f_L = \dfrac{f_0}{Q_s}.
$$
